Agent Swarm can prepare a source-linked competitor change brief for people to review: configured shared agents gather the public pages your team names, record the source and the date of each observation, and assemble one briefing that sales and leadership read before a call. What that takes off the team is the gathering and the comparing. Someone still judges what a change means, and a named person approves each claim before it reaches a prospect.

The scope is competitor change research for sales and leadership inside one company. A change in an external rule or a regulation is a policy review. A proposed change to customer pricing is a finance question. General prospecting is covered in [Lead prospecting](/docs/playbooks/lead-prospecting), and reading your own delivery and quality records in [the supplier performance review guide](/docs/playbooks/use-cases/supplier-performance-review). This guide covers what a competitor published in public, not your own customers, suppliers or policies.

## When another tool fits better

**A competitive-intelligence service, when you need continuous coverage.** If someone must hear about a competitor's price page the day it moves, across twenty competitors, and have it arrive as an alert with a curated history behind it, a dedicated product does that work. Coverage, alerting and a maintained dataset are its job.

**Manual review, when the set is small.** Two or three competitors whose sites one owner reads every month is a task a person handles in a sitting. A workflow adds a queue and a gate to a job that needs neither.

Agent Swarm fits a different shape: several people need the same view, and a wrong claim sent to a prospect costs more than the gathering. It provides no competitor database, no alerting product and no real-time monitoring.

## An illustrative workflow

This is an illustrative design: not a built-in competitor feature, not a monitoring integration, not a measured deployment and not a customer result. Collection, prior-version storage and the comparison logic are custom work your team writes and tests. The pieces it assembles are documented; the arrangement is yours.

**1. The team picks the sources and a cadence.** Decide which public pages count: pricing, product, changelog, public documentation. Name the paths rather than the whole domain, so a redesign does not flood the brief. [Scheduling](/docs/concepts/scheduling) runs the workflow on the cadence and records each run.

**2. Collection records the evidence.** A `swarm-script` step reaches each named source and writes JSON carrying the source URL and the observation date for every item. Keeping the model out of this step is what makes two runs comparable. See [Scripts runtime](/docs/guides/scripts-runtime), [Script connections](/docs/guides/script-connections) and [Reports from multiple sources](/docs/playbooks/reports-multiple-sources).

**3. A reviewer step compares the run with the stored prior version.** Beside the last stored version it marks what changed, what disappeared and what it could not read. Storing the prior version and comparing runs is custom code your team owns. Nothing in Agent Swarm watches a page for you; this is a script you maintain, not a built-in change detector.

**4. An agent drafts the brief.** The agent turns the compared JSON into a briefing for sales and leadership: what changed, what it means for a conversation, and which source and date each line rests on. Every line carries its link and its observation date, so a reader can check the wording. The drafting step is an ordinary agent task inside the [workflow](/docs/concepts/workflows).

**5. A gate pauses the workflow.** The brief lands in [agent-fs](https://github.com/desplega-ai/agent-fs) as a file a person can keep. A [human-in-the-loop gate](/docs/playbooks/patterns/hitl-gates) blocks the workflow, posts to Slack with a summary and the file link, and resumes only when a named person responds. The gate carries a timeout and a fallback, and the workflow never proceeds on silence. The `human-in-the-loop` executor blocks; `request-human-input` is the separate route for asking a person a question.

**6. A person verifies before anything leaves the team.** Each claim is checked against the source it names before the brief is quoted in a deck or an email. Until then it stays internal. [Lead prospecting](/docs/playbooks/lead-prospecting) holds the same shape for outbound: agents draft, and nothing sends without an approval.

## What the brief flags

Five conditions belong in the brief, each with its source and left unresolved.

- **A source that could not be reached.** A page returned an error or redirected to a login form. The brief names the URL and says the item is unknown, not unchanged.
- **A source older than the cadence window.** A page last observed three weeks ago on a weekly cadence. The observation date tells a reader how old the claim is.
- **A page that changed while its meaning stayed unclear.** The diff cannot tell a wording edit from a price change, so both versions are shown.
- **A claim with only one source.** A feature appears on a product page and nowhere in the public documentation. One source is recorded as one source.
- **An item missing compared with the prior version.** A section that existed last month and is gone. The brief names who can confirm it.

Leaving these open is the point. A brief that picks the friendlier reading of an ambiguous page hands a salesperson a claim they cannot defend.

## What stays human-owned

Five decisions stay with people.

- **Choosing the sources.** Which pages count as a competitor signal is a judgement about your market.
- **Checking that access respects each site.** Terms of service, robots rules and rate limits apply, and a person confirms the collection honours them.
- **Verifying each claim.** Reading the source and deciding whether the page supports the sentence in the brief is a call with a name on it.
- **Approving external use.** Any claim that goes into a sales conversation or a public comparison is approved by someone accountable for it.
- **Owning the cadence.** Whether a weekly brief is enough, or a change needs one that day, is a decision the team records.

The agent makes none of these calls and has no path to a prospect. Gate the step that cannot be undone: gathering pages and drafting a brief are reversible, and a claim repeated to a prospect is not.

## Limitations

**There is no real-time monitoring.** Collection runs on the cadence you set, and the brief reports when each page was last observed so the lag is visible.

**There is no automatic change detection unless you build it.** Where the diff is naive, a copy edit and a price change look alike, and the brief says so.

**Access to third-party sites is not unrestricted.** Login walls, robots rules, rate limits and terms of service apply. A page behind a login is one the brief cannot read, and the collection reports it as unreachable.

**The brief is only as complete as the sources you configure.** A roadmap in a conference talk or a reseller's listing sits outside a list of owned URLs. Where a signal lives somewhere you did not name, the brief is silent, and silence is not evidence that nothing changed.

## Review checklist

- **Sources.** Each source has a URL, a stated section, and a person who confirmed it may be read.
- **Freshness.** Every line carries an observation date, and the brief states its cadence.
- **Gaps.** Unreachable, stale and ambiguous items are listed as open with their source, not filled by inference.
- **Verification.** A named person has checked each claim against its source before it leaves the team.
- **Approval.** State who approves external use and what happens when nobody answers. Silence is never approval.
- **Ownership.** The cadence and the diff script have an owner and a backup.

## Building blocks

Each piece ships in agent-swarm and is documented. Pointing it at competitor pages, and writing the collection and comparison code, is yours.

- [Scheduling](/docs/concepts/scheduling): cron and interval schedules, and a record of each run.
- [Workflows](/docs/concepts/workflows): the steps, the `swarm-script` node that emits JSON, and the agent step that drafts the brief.
- [Reports from multiple sources](/docs/playbooks/reports-multiple-sources): deterministic collection that emits JSON, then an agent that synthesises it.
- [HITL gates](/docs/playbooks/patterns/hitl-gates): the blocking `human-in-the-loop` node, the approve and reject branches, and the timeout.
- [Scripts runtime](/docs/guides/scripts-runtime) and [script connections](/docs/guides/script-connections): what a collection script may call, and how an API is registered once.
- [Lead prospecting](/docs/playbooks/lead-prospecting) and [supplier performance review](/docs/playbooks/use-cases/supplier-performance-review): the same draft, gate and human-decision shape, applied to outbound and to your own records.
